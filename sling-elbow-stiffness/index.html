Elbow Stiffness and the Sling: Your Questions Answered

By James Park · October 2026

If you've had shoulder surgery and your elbow feels tight or sore, you're not imagining it. The sling that protects your shoulder also locks your elbow in one position for weeks, and several hospital patient-information sheets describe the resulting stiffness as an expected part of recovery rather than a complication.

Why does my elbow feel stiff after shoulder surgery?

It's mostly a side effect of the sling, not the shoulder repair itself. Central Queensland Upper Limb notes that a sling fitted correctly holds the elbow at roughly 90 degrees, and it stays there for most of the day and night for weeks at a time. A joint that doesn't move for that long can tighten up, even though the elbow was never operated on.

Is the sling actually to blame, or is this just normal healing?

Both, in a sense — the sling is the mechanism, and the stiffness it causes is considered a known, acceptable trade-off. A patient leaflet from Sandwell and West Birmingham Hospitals NHS Trust states plainly that continual sling wear "can increase the risk of stiffness and reduced movement occurring at the shoulder and elbow." The same leaflet also warns that skipping the sling against advice can leave the shoulder repair itself worse off, so the stiffness is generally treated as the lesser problem.

Tip

Stiffness that's limited to the bend of the elbow, and that loosens up once you start moving the arm again, is usually not something to worry about on its own.

Should I be worried that something has gone wrong?

In most cases, no — the sheets reviewed here treat elbow stiffness as routine, not as a red flag. NHS Norfolk & Waveney says the crease of the elbow may feel "a little sore and tight" after being "held in a bent position" for around six weeks, and adds that this tends to ease once the arm returns to normal use. It's worth noting that research tying long immobilization to elbow stiffness mainly comes from trauma patients in casts, not shoulder-sling wearers — a case-control study of 688 patients found longer cast time might raise stiffness risk after elbow injuries specifically, which is a different situation from a healthy elbow resting in a sling.

How often should I actually move my elbow while wearing a sling?

Several sources converge on at least a few times a day, with two giving an exact number. shoulderdoc.co.uk and the St Vincent's occupational therapy guide both instruct patients to loosen the sling and move the elbow, wrist, and hand "at least 4 times per day." WashU Medicine Orthopedics and Central Queensland Upper Limb use softer wording — "several times a day" — but the message is the same: the elbow isn't meant to stay frozen the whole time you're wearing the sling.

What's different if I also had a biceps tenodesis?

The advice flips in some important ways, because the biceps tendon crosses the elbow. Summit Orthopedics lists "no elbow flexion or supination against resistance until 6 weeks post-op" as an early precaution, and the Ohio State Wexner guideline goes further, allowing only passive elbow movement in the first two weeks and recommending a towel placed under the elbow at night specifically to prevent full extension. If you had a tenodesis alongside your cuff repair, generic "straighten your elbow" advice may not apply to you.

Watch out

If you had a biceps tenodesis, check your own surgical protocol before doing any active elbow stretching — the movement rules can be the opposite of those given after a standalone cuff repair.

Why does my elbow bother me more at night than during the day?

Nighttime is usually the longest stretch the elbow spends bent without a break, and most protocols keep the sling on for sleep. WashU Medicine Orthopedics tells patients to wear the sling overnight, then suggests tucking a pillow behind the elbow, noting it "often helps with the pain." The Sandwell leaflet gives similar guidance for side or back sleepers: placing a folded pillow under the elbow of the operated arm for support. Some patients also use a dedicated support cushion, such as the Flexicomfort Shoulder Pillow, to hold the forearm in roughly that position without having to improvise with bedding. A 2025 meta-analysis of sleep quality after rotator cuff repair found scores improved substantially by six months, though stiffness was named as one factor that can keep some patients sleeping poorly in the meantime.

When does elbow stiffness usually go away?

There's no fixed week count in the sources reviewed — the consistent message is that it eases as you return to normal arm use rather than resolving on a set schedule. NHS Norfolk & Waveney describes a gradual weaning process, such as spending an hour out of the sling one day and two hours the next, as the stiffness and soreness fade together. A 2026 review of upper-limb stiffness explains the underlying mechanism as capsule and tendon fibrosis from prolonged immobilization — though that review focuses on elbow surgery patients, not sling wearers, so it may not translate directly to typical post-shoulder-surgery stiffness.

When should I call my surgeon or physiotherapist about elbow pain?

Call if the symptoms go beyond ordinary stiffness — for example, a hand that turns cold, blue, or numb and doesn't improve when the sling is loosened, or pain that medication doesn't touch. Central Queensland Upper Limb lists both of these as reasons to contact your care team rather than wait it out. Persistent or worsening symptoms are best assessed by a doctor or physiotherapist rather than managed on your own.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal for my elbow to hurt after shoulder surgery?

Yes, it's commonly reported. Hospital leaflets describe elbow stiffness as a known risk of wearing a sling continuously, and some patients notice a sore, tight feeling at the crease after several weeks bent. It's generally considered a side effect of immobilization rather than a sign of a problem with the shoulder repair itself.

Can I take my elbow out of the sling to stretch it?

Usually, yes, within the limits your surgeon sets. Several sheets recommend moving the elbow, wrist, and hand multiple times a day while still protecting the shoulder. If you had a biceps tenodesis, though, your allowed movements may be more restricted, so check your specific instructions first.

How many times a day should I move my elbow?

Guidance varies slightly, but at least four times a day is a figure that appears in more than one source. Others simply say "several times a day." None of the sources treat elbow movement as optional during sling wear.

Will a pillow help with elbow discomfort at night?

It may. Hospital guidance suggests placing a folded pillow under or behind the elbow while lying down, which some patients find reduces nighttime discomfort. This is offered as a comfort measure for positioning, not as a treatment for the underlying stiffness.

Does elbow stiffness mean my shoulder repair is failing?

There's nothing in the sources reviewed linking elbow stiffness to the success of a shoulder repair. It's consistently framed as a consequence of the sling holding the arm still, and it's expected to improve as you resume normal arm use — though any sharp changes in pain or sensation are worth discussing with a doctor.
